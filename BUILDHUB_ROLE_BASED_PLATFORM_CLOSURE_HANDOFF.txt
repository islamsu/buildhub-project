2. SECURITY FINDINGS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

FOUND AND FIXED IN THIS CLOSURE PASS

  C1  FABRICATED RATINGS ON REAL, NAMED COMPANIES.  The worst thing in the
      repository, and it was not a security hole - it was an honesty one.
      client/src/lib/marketplaceData.ts held VENDORS, DESIGNERS and
      FINISHING_COMPANIES with invented ratings, review counts, project counts,
      years of experience, team sizes, award badges and `verified: true`.
      Several entries were REAL Egyptian businesses - Ezz Steel, Elsewedy
      Electric, Ceramica Cleopatra, Jotun Egypt - with no BuildHub account and
      no knowledge of the platform. A 4.9 beside a real company is a factual
      claim about a third party; a "verified" badge is a claim BuildHub was
      making about a business it had verified nothing about.

      Rendered on FOUR surfaces: /marketplace/designers, /marketplace/finishing,
      and the Marketplace Hub's three featured strips AND its autocomplete. An
      earlier phase converted /marketplace/vendors and /marketplace/products to
      real data and left these four behind - the leftover half being the one
      naming real companies.

      All four now read marketplace.vendors. An absent rating renders as "no
      reviews yet", never 0 and never a plausible number. An empty marketplace
      shows an empty marketplace.

  C2  MESSAGE ATTACHMENTS NEVER WORKED, AND REPAIRING THEM NAIVELY WOULD HAVE
      OPENED AN IDOR.  messages.send declared `fileUrl: z.string().url()`;
      storagePut returns a RELATIVE proxy path and the client sends exactly
      that back, so zod refused every real attachment. It was also free-form
      client input naming a storage key, and the proxy authorizes a message
      attachment by MATCHING that stored path - so a message referencing
      someone else's key would have handed its sender that file. Not
      exploitable only because the absolute URL zod demanded could never equal
      the relative path the proxy compares. Fixing the feature alone would have
      created the hole. Now constrained to a message-attachment path owned by
      the sender's own session id.

  C3  admin.fullAuditReport READ EVERY COLUMN OF EVERY USER - passwordHash,
      invitationToken, openId - into memory to build an export. Nothing leaked;
      the projection below it is an allowlist. It is the exact pattern
      ADMIN_USER_LIST_COLUMNS exists to forbid, one endpoint away, and the next
      person to write `...targetUser` would have shipped it. Now a nine-column
      list, and the rule is ENFORCED: every select().from(users) must be on a
      twelve-entry allowlist naming why the full row is genuinely needed.

  C4  THE WINNER WAS TOLD THEY LOST.  Accepting a quotation auto-rejects the
      others and notifies their providers - by mapping every other quotation
      ROW to a recipient, not every other provider. A provider who bid three
      times and won received "Quotation not selected" twice, seconds after
      "Quotation accepted".

  C5  THREE NOTIFICATIONS WERE INVISIBLE TO THEIR OWN COVERAGE TEST. The
      Phase 1B localisation test read routers.ts alone and never opened
      quotationWorkflow.ts, so its three calls stayed English-only while the
      rule reported a clean pass. The scan now walks every non-test server
      source, counts per file, and names the offender.

  C6  THE LEAD CREDIT BOUGHT A TOAST. Supplier -> Qualified Enquiries -> View
      details spent a credit; the server returned the full RFQ in that same
      response and the client discarded it. It is the ONLY way a vendor can
      read that detail - rfq.get is requester-scoped. Now rendered.

FOUND AND FIXED EARLIER IN THIS ENGAGEMENT (re-verified, section 3)

  P1  auth.updateRole accepted userRole:'admin' from any authenticated user.
  P2  admin.createUser could mint a role='admin' row under `users.manage`.
  P3  A quotation could be submitted against a closed or awarded RFQ.
  P4  A NEGATIVE quotation price was accepted (decimal(12,2), unbounded).
  P5  LIKE wildcards came straight from user input on four search paths.
  P6  marketplace.list, public and unauthenticated, had no limit at all.
  P7  Three role workspace cards described work the product cannot do.
  P8  Notifications were stored as English prose in a bilingual product.
  P9  marketplace.askQuestion let ten product ids skip validation.
  P10 "Storage not configured" was reported as a 500.

REMAINING - NOT FIXED, AND WHY

  R1  PRODUCT IMAGES DO NOT EXIST. products.images is a column nothing writes;
      there is no upload procedure and the storage proxy has no product-images
      category. A supplier cannot attach a photograph to a listing.
  R2  A SUPPLIER CANNOT EDIT OR DELETE A LISTING. There is no
      marketplace.update and no delete anywhere in the product.
  R3  AN RFQ CANNOT BE AMENDED after posting, for the same reason.
  R4  NO EXPORT of reports or analytics, and no invoice or receipt.
  R5  PROJECT-AWARE AI IS NOT IMPLEMENTED (section 13).
  R6  DUPLICATE QUOTATIONS - OWNER DECISION, section 12.

  R1-R4 are ABSENT PRODUCT SURFACE, not defects to patch. Adding mandatory
  fields, an edit path or an image pipeline is new product with real decisions
  behind it (what is mandatory? what happens to a live listing when it is
  edited?), and inventing those was explicitly out of bounds.

WHAT THE MATRIX MAKES VISIBLE, which is why it was worth writing out:

  - BuildHub HAS ALMOST NO DELETE SURFACE. Outside ai.deleteAttachment and a
    vendor replacing their declared service categories, nothing in the product
    can be deleted by anyone - not an RFQ, a quotation, a message or a project.
  - NO EDIT either, for most things. The whole router has exactly two update
    procedures: projects.update and profile.update.
  - THERE IS NO ORDER AND NO CHECKOUT.
  - ENGINEER, ARCHITECT AND PROJECT MANAGER HAVE IDENTICAL AUTHORITY. They
    differ in EXPERIENCE - dashboard, AI tools, prompts, workflows - not in
    permissions. The test asserts three distinct signatures rather than six,
    because manufacturing a difference to make the table look richer would be
    the same dishonesty as the fabricated vendors.
  - THE ONLY SUPPLIER-ONLY CAPABILITY IS THE PRODUCT CATALOGUE.

6. DATA ISOLATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

server/dataIsolationMatrix.test.ts enumerates EVERY procedure that accepts an
id and requires each to be owner-scoped, admin-gated, or named in a
ten-entry allowlist WITH ITS REASON. The property is not that today's 62 are
right; it is that tomorrow's 63rd cannot be quietly wrong.

Ownership is recognised in the four shapes this codebase actually uses -
predicate, insert value, fetch-then-compare, and passed-to-a-helper - because
the first version of the rule knew two and flagged three CORRECTLY scoped
procedures.

Two further rules scan the WHOLE surface, not just id-takers: no procedure may
read an owner id from the request, and none may write an owner column from it.
That second scope was itself a hole a mutation found.

CROSS-ORGANIZATION ACCESS IS NOT APPLICABLE. BuildHub has no organization,
company or tenant concept - every account is an individual. Rather than claim a
control that cannot exist, the suite asserts the ABSENCE: add such a column and
the test fails until every owner-scoped query is extended.

8. RFQ
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

submitQuotation reads the RFQ FIRST and refuses a closed or awarded one with
CONFLICT, and a missing one with NOT_FOUND rather than a 500 from the foreign
key. Bounds match the columns. The client's status filter is no longer the only
thing standing between a bidder and an awarded RFQ.

State machine: open -> awarded (accept, which auto-rejects every other bid in
one row-locked transaction) or open -> closed. 'cancelled' and 'expired' DO NOT
EXIST in the enum; the matrix and this handoff say so rather than implying a
richer lifecycle than the product has.

12. OWNER DECISIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

D1  DUPLICATE QUOTATIONS.  Investigated before touching anything, as asked.

    WHAT THE REPOSITORY ESTABLISHES: submission is UNMETERED.
    BUILDHUB_PHASE4A_MONETIZATION_ARCHITECTURE.md, twice: "any approved
    provider can submit unlimited quotations to unlimited RFQs at no cost" and
    "unlimited free quotation submission" as the current vendor proposition.

    WHAT IT ESTABLISHES ABOUT REVISIONS: nothing. No status beyond
    pending/accepted/rejected, no supersededBy, no version, no history table,
    no unique index on (rfqId, providerId), no update procedure, no UI that
    blocks a second bid. "Unlimited" there is about QUOTA - we do not charge
    per lead - not about bidding twice on one RFQ.

    THEREFORE: OWNER DECISION REQUIRED. The workflow is unchanged.

    NO LEAK OR OWNERSHIP CORRUPTION, verified: every row carries providerId
    from the session, the requester is entitled to every bid they see, and
    accepting one auto-rejects all others in the same transaction. The one real
    harm duplicates caused - the winner being told they lost, once per extra
    bid - is fixed, because addressing each provider once is correct under
    EITHER answer.

    KNOWN DISTORTION IF DUPLICATES CONTINUE: analytics.myStats computes win
    rate as accepted/submitted. Five bids on one RFQ and one win reads as 20%.

    For consideration, NOT implemented: one active quotation per provider per
    RFQ, with controlled revisions and history. Needs a decision on what
    happens to a revision after the requester has read the original.

D2  'Renovation' AS THE FINISHING DIRECTORY'S CATEGORY. The shared taxonomy has
    no "finishing" value. A one-word change if the answer is different.

D3  MANDATORY PRODUCT FIELDS. Today a listing needs a name and a category and
    goes live immediately - no price, no image, no lead time. Which fields
    should be mandatory, and whether an incomplete product should be a draft,
    is a product decision, not one to infer from a zod schema.

ROLE-BASED PLATFORM - GO. Six roles with distinct experiences and a matrix that
is checked against the router rather than written down. Authorization is
server-side everywhere it matters: the marketplace role is user-chosen and
grants nothing, the authorization column is separate and untouchable from the
client, and provider surfaces stay closed until an administrator approves. The
gaps that remain are ABSENT SURFACE - no delete, no edit, no product images, no
export - not broken controls, and they are named in section 2.

STAGING - GO. 621/621 at the exact SHA under test, five environment skips, zero
failures.

PRODUCTION - NO-GO, and this is engineering's recommendation, not a blocker
anybody imposed. Four things stand in the way, none of them in this phase's
scope:

  1. NO EMAIL. SMTP is unconfigured, so password reset does not work and no
     notification leaves the platform. A marketplace where a quotation arrives
     and nobody is told is not a marketplace.
  2. NO OBJECT STORAGE. Every attachment path degrades honestly, but degrades.
  3. NO PAYMENT INTEGRATION. Plans and entitlements are real; there is no way
     to pay for one.
  4. THE OWNER DECISIONS IN SECTION 12 are open, and D1 shapes how bidding
     works.

Production also remains a separate architecture (Vultr) that this staging
environment deliberately shares nothing with.
