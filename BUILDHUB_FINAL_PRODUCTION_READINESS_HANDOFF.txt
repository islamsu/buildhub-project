3.  DEFECTS FOUND AND FIXED THIS SESSION
================================================================================

P1-01  AN RFQ COULD BE CREATED THAT NO SUPPLIER COULD EVER ANSWER.

  `openQualifiedEnquiry` refuses any RFQ whose category is not in
  RFQ_CATEGORIES. Creation accepted `category: z.string().optional()` and the
  submit button was guarded only on the title.

  So a customer could post a request that was stored, listed in the feed every
  supplier sees, and permanently impossible to open or quote on. Proven live:
  create returned 200, openEnquiry returned 403 FORBIDDEN, forever. The
  customer waits for responses that cannot arrive; suppliers see a request
  they are forbidden to answer; nobody is told.

  FIXED. Category required and typed to the taxonomy on both sides. Not a
  business decision — the eligibility rule needing it already existed. A
  free-text category failed identically ("Marble" is not a member), so the
  enum closes both holes.

P1-02  THE FEED'S RESPOND BUTTON WENT TO A DASHBOARD, NOT TO THE REQUEST.

  `<Link href="/provider">` on every RFQ card — while the card's own title
  link, three lines above, already pointed at `/rfq/:id`. A supplier clicking
  "Respond" on one specific request landed on a generic workspace with no
  memory of which one. Exactly the "generic dashboard" failure your §8 names.

  FIXED. It goes to the review page. The guardian that should have caught it
  asserted "the respond route resolves" — and `/provider` resolves perfectly.
  Resolving and carrying the user to the right place are different properties.

P1-03  A SUPPLIER COULD NOT SEND A SINGLE DOCUMENT WITH A BID.

  `quotations` had no attachments column, `submitQuotation` had no attachments
  input, the quote dialog had four fields. No proposal, no specification, no
  certificate, no photograph. Your §11 calls this mandatory; it did not exist.

  BUILT. Column, migration, upload procedure, storage category, supplier form,
  customer review surface, audit detail.

  THE AUTHORIZATION DIRECTION IS THE OPPOSITE OF AN RFQ ATTACHMENT and that is
  the design. An RFQ attachment flows customer→provider and is what a credit
  BUYS. A quotation attachment flows provider→customer and is sold to nobody:
  the value of a sealed bid is that rivals cannot read it. Sharing the
  `rfq-attachments/` prefix would have inherited "any provider who paid for
  the enquiry" and published every supplier's bid to every rival on the lead.

P2-04  A TEST HELPER WAS SILENTLY DELETING THE CODE UNDER TEST.

  Twelve test files stripped comments with `replace(/\/\*[\s\S]*?\*\//g, '')`.
  `accept="image/*,application/pdf"` contains `/*`. In RFQPage.tsx that opened
  a false comment which swallowed 6,045 characters including the submit button.

  The failures that shout are harmless. The dangerous case is
  `expect(source).not.toContain(x)`, which passes on text deleted before
  anybody looked — a guardian asserting something bad is ABSENT cannot tell
  absent from never examined. I checked the one that mattered: the
  `not.toContain('openEnquiry')` assertion on RFQPage is genuinely true under
  both strippers, so that invariant held.

  FIXED. All twelve migrated to a scanner that tracks string literals, unit
  tested with a negative control proving the old idiom really does lose code.

P2-05  THREE CONTROLS HAD NO ACCESSIBLE NAME.

  The notifications button and the mobile menu toggle (icon-only, announced as
  "button"), and the marketplace search field (named only by a placeholder,
  which vanishes once there is text in it). The mobile toggle is the only way
  to navigate on a phone. FIXED, bilingually, with aria-expanded on the toggle.

P3-06  A FIXED-WINDOW TEST HELPER REPORTED AN ABSENCE IT HAD NOT ESTABLISHED.

  `procedureBody` returned a 2,500-character slice; adding the attachments
  schema pushed `rfq.status !== 'open'` past the edge and the test reported the
  status check missing when it was three lines below. FIXED to bound on the
  next procedure.

  · "the pre-reset session is invalidated" asserted on `me.data.username`.

    load at all ("attachments are not shown" is trivially true on a blank page).

  indicator". It has one. I measured `outlineStyle`, which is `none` by design
  — the buttons draw focus with a Tailwind ring, a box-shadow. Checked
  properly, all 23 focusable elements have a visible indicator. A false P2 in a
  readiness report costs someone a day chasing a problem that is not there.

8.  OWNER DECISIONS — STILL OPEN
================================================================================

  D1  DUPLICATE QUOTATIONS.  ← the one that touches money
      CURRENT: nothing stops one supplier submitting several quotations on one
      RFQ; each notifies the requester again.
      PROBLEM: win-rate reporting counts rows, not attempts. One win against
      five duplicate bids from the same supplier reads as 20% when commercially
      it was one attempt.
      OPTIONS: (A) one active quotation per supplier per RFQ, later submissions
      rejected; (B) revisions — a new submission supersedes and the history is
      kept; (C) multiple independent bids, and reporting counts DISTINCT
      suppliers.
      RECOMMENDED: (B). It matches how construction bidding actually works,
      keeps an audit trail, and fixes the reporting distortion without
      forbidding a supplier from correcting a price.
      IMPACT: a `supersededBy` or `revision` column plus a unique partial
      index; the comparison surface shows the current revision with history;
      analytics count distinct suppliers. Not implemented — this is a
      commercial rule and I will not invent it.

  D2  Renovation finishing preset.
  D3  Mandatory product fields before a listing may publish.
  D4  Comparison surface — is provider identity revealed before award?
  D5  RFQ budget visibility to providers (the open feed currently shows it).
  D6  Messaging relationship rule — who may message whom, and after what.
