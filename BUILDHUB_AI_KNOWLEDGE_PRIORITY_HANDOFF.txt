============================================================
WHERE THE CONTENT LIVES - DETERMINED, NOT ASSUMED
============================================================

There was NO retrieval capability of any kind in the codebase. I checked
before designing anything.

The authoritative content is not in pages or a CMS. It lives in the modules
that ENFORCE the rules:

  shared/billing.ts       PLANS - prices, entitlements, trial and grace
                          periods - and ENTITLEMENT_ENFORCEMENT, which records
                          which entitlements are actually enforced today
  shared/compliance.ts    COMPLIANCE_REQUIREMENTS: the documents each vendor
                          role must submit for approval. Already bilingual.
  server/routers.ts       the workflow rules that exist as CODE PATHS rather
                          than constants: who may quote, what the RFQ feed
                          shows, how a qualified-enquiry credit is consumed
  server/vendorDirectory.ts  featured placement vs organic ranking
  client/src/contexts/LanguageContext.tsx  1,520 bilingual copy keys - the
                          website's own words, in both languages

Nothing else claims to be authoritative. There is no FAQ page, no help centre,
no terms page and no privacy page in this application (see GAPS).

============================================================
ARCHITECTURE - DERIVED, NOT COPIED
============================================================

  server/_core/buildhubKnowledge.ts   assembles the briefing at CALL TIME
  shared/platformRules.ts             the prose rules, each tied to its
                                      enforcing file

Prices, allowances, entitlements and document lists are READ from the same
constants the application enforces, every request. Change 499 to 549 in
billing.ts and the assistant says 549 on the next question - no prompt to
rewrite, no knowledge base to re-sync, no re-embedding. That is the
maintainability requirement, and it is enforced by a test that FAILS if a
price literal ever appears in the knowledge module.

The rules that are not constants could not be derived, so they are written
once in shared/platformRules.ts - and each one carries the file that enforces
it plus a literal that must still appear in it. A test reads those paths. If
`submitQuotation` stops being an `approvedProviderProcedure`, the rule saying
"only approved vendors may quote" fails rather than quietly becoming fiction.

That is the answer to "do not create a fake knowledge base": every sentence
the assistant may state about BuildHub is anchored to code that enforces it,
and the anchor is checked.

HONESTY IS BUILT IN. Four of the nine entitlements are defined in the plan
table but enforced by nothing. Rather than advertising them, each is marked in
the briefing:

  visibilityLevel: boosted (DEFINED BUT NOT YET ENFORCED - do not describe
  this as a working feature)

SOURCE PRIORITY, AS IMPLEMENTED
============================================================

The system prompt states the hierarchy explicitly and the briefing follows it:

  1. AUTHORITATIVE BUILDHUB INFORMATION - for anything about BuildHub
  2. general construction expertise - for construction itself

with the rules the assistant may not break: BuildHub wins over general
knowledge; never assume BuildHub works like another marketplace; never invent
a policy, feature, price, limit or process; say plainly when BuildHub has not
specified something and label general guidance AS general guidance.

PRIORITY 2 in your hierarchy - live structured application data such as
products and vendor records - is NOT implemented, deliberately, and I am
flagging that rather than pretending otherwise. Supplying database rows to the
model needs a per-row authorization design, and the safe version of that is a
larger piece of work than this task authorised. What exists today is
product-rule grounding. See GAPS.

asking the model to behave.
