11. RFQ BASKET — DEFECT D1, THE LARGEST GAP IN THE PRODUCT
================================================================================

  WHAT WAS THERE. On the marketplace grid, "Add to RFQ list" ran

      onClick={event => { event.stopPropagation();
                          toast.success('Added to RFQ list'); }}

  and did nothing else. LIVE PROOF: localStorage byte-identical before and
  after the click, on all twelve product cards. A success message for an action
  that did not occur.

  On the product page it wrote ONE localStorage key with setItem. LIVE PROOF:
  add product 1 → {"productId":1,...}; add product 2 → {"productId":2,...}.
  The first was gone, under a button labelled "list" / "قائمة".

  And the RFQ could not represent items at all: no items table, no quantity, no
  unit, no per-item specification. productReference held one product id and was
  never checked against the products table.

  WHAT IS THERE NOW. A real basket, and rfqItems rows written in the same
  transaction as the RFQ. Catalogue name, unit and price are RE-READ from the
  products table and never taken from the client — a basket is editable in
  localStorage, so without this a request could read "Premium Italian Marble"
  while carrying a cement product's id. A product that does not exist, or has
  been WITHDRAWN, is refused rather than silently dropped.

  LIVE VERIFIED 19/19, every step §7 lists:
    browse → add from the grid → add two more from product pages with real
    quantities → refresh → navigate away → return → review → change a quantity
    to 7 → add a specification → remove a line → submit → TWO rfqItems ROWS IN
    THE DATABASE carrying the edited quantity and the specification, each
    linked to a real product → the RFQ detail page renders them with quantities
    → basket emptied only after the RFQ exists.
    Negative: withdrawn product refused; nonexistent product refused.

19. BATCH PRODUCT IMPORT — OWNER REQUIRED IT, SO IT IS BUILT
================================================================================

  The previous audit recorded it as NOT IMPLEMENTED and left it there. Asked
  directly, the owner made it a launch requirement.

  WHAT THE FILE IS NOT ALLOWED TO DECIDE:
    WHO OWNS THE ROWS. Every row is written with the caller's supplierId. A
      supplierId column in the CSV is dropped at PARSE time — the parser emits
      only known columns. That is where the defence actually lives, and it is
      pinned there by a test, because mutating the insert to read
      row.supplierId is a no-op precisely because of it.
    WHAT A CATEGORY MEANS. It must be in the shared taxonomy or the row fails.
    WHETHER A PARTIAL IMPORT IS ACCEPTABLE. It is not. One bad row writes
      nothing: a partial import leaves a supplier unable to tell what landed,
      and re-uploading then duplicates whatever did.

  The CSV reader handles quoted commas, escaped quotes, embedded newlines, the
  BOM Excel writes and Arabic-Indic digits. A naive comma split silently
  corrupts any Arabic description containing a comma.

  LIVE VERIFIED 20/20 through the real file input: three products with Arabic
  names imported; the Arabic and a quoted comma survived the round trip; a bad
  file reported "Row 3 · name — Name is required" and "Row 4 · category —
  NotACategory is not a BuildHub category" and wrote NOTHING; a homeowner is
  refused; a supplierId column left the homeowner owning zero products.

  MY OWN TEMPLATE shipped an invalid category. The test that parses the
  template caught it before it shipped.

61. FINAL HUMAN-READABLE SUMMARY
================================================================================

WHAT WAS MISSING
----------------
The RFQ basket, entirely. A customer could not ask for two things in one
request — there was no items table, no quantity, no unit, no specification —
while two buttons in the product told them they had just added something to a
list. Bulk product import, so a supplier with a real catalogue had no way in.

WHAT WAS BROKEN
----------------
One homeowner could read another homeowner's full brief, exact budget and
location, and so could any supplier. A product could be listed under any string
at all as its category, which made it unfindable through the only filter the
marketplace offers. The marketplace "Add to RFQ" button showed a success
message and did nothing. The product page's version held exactly one product
and silently destroyed the first when a second was added.

WHAT WAS FIXED
---------------
A real basket, producing real rfqItems rows in the same transaction as the RFQ,
with catalogue names re-read server-side and withdrawn products refused. The
RFQ feed narrowed to approved providers and the requester. One shared product
taxonomy enforced on every write path. Bulk CSV import with preview, row-level
errors, Arabic support, duplicate detection and all-or-nothing commit. Plus,
from earlier in the engagement: the CSP that was blocking every stored image,
the four fabricated figures and three invented testimonials on the front page,
notifications that pointed nowhere, and a 676 KB → 187 KB first load.

WHAT WAS ACTUALLY LIVE VERIFIED
--------------------------------
The complete commercial journey, 23/23. The RFQ basket, 19/19. Bulk import and
the narrowed feed, 20/20. Isolation and notification recipients, 40/40. Arabic
across six pages, 30/30. Catalogue, search and image security, 19/20. Responsive
layout at three widths, 51/55. Cache isolation across a real Sign Out and a
second sign-in, 5/5. All against a real browser and a real MariaDB.

WHAT WAS ONLY UNIT/STATIC VERIFIED
-----------------------------------
Quotation reject and expiry paths. Concurrency, under a lock simulation rather
than real contention. The audit-event trail. Product edit. Most admin
procedures beyond the three probed live. The AI surfaces were not re-proven at
all in this audit and are recorded as NOT VERIFIED rather than inherited.

WHAT IS STILL NOT IMPLEMENTED
-----------------------------
Quotation revision by a supplier (tied to the duplicate-quotation decision).
Message deep links to a specific conversation. Nothing else material was found
absent that the product claims to have.

WHAT IS BLOCKED BY INFRASTRUCTURE
---------------------------------
Staging verification of any kind — egress is blocked from this environment.
Real SMTP. Real object storage. Real payments. None was faked, no TLS was
disabled, and no result from a local stand-in is reported as a provider result.

WHAT REQUIRES OWNER DECISION
----------------------------
Testimonials, the small public counters, duplicate quotations (recommended:
allow revision), visibilityLevel (recommended: leave unenforced), the messaging
relationship policy, and response editing.

WHAT MUST HAPPEN BEFORE PRODUCTION
----------------------------------
Deploy 264353f to staging and verify the SHA. Re-run the five live suites
there. Confirm a product photo renders against real object storage. Send one
real password-reset email. Decide the three commercial owner items.

FINAL VERDICT
-------------
PRODUCTION NO-GO — on three of twenty conditions, all three of them
infrastructure verification that this environment cannot perform, none of them
a code defect.

The product is in materially better shape than when this audit began: a feature
its own buttons promised now exists, and a customer can no longer read another
customer's budget. Neither of those was in the previous "final" report, which
is the whole argument for this one. But I will not call it GO while the thing
real users will touch has never been tested, and the difference between "it
works here" and "it works there" is exactly the difference the last two P1s
lived in.
