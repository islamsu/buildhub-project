PART 1 — WHAT THE AUDIT FOUND
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The repository was audited directly rather than from the previous handoffs.
Most of the brief was already implemented across earlier phases. THREE things
were genuinely absent, and two of them were built this phase:

  GAP 1 (BUILT)   No untrusted-content boundary anywhere. Vendor-authored text
                  reached the model in the same channel as BuildHub's own
                  instructions, raw. A real injection vector, not theoretical.
  GAP 2 (BUILT)   No transaction opportunity engine. The AI could recommend
                  providers to customers and had nothing for providers.
  GAP 3 (NOT BUILT) No audit-trail table. See PART P.

Two commits landed. Everything else described below was verified as already
present and is reported with measured figures, not restated from a handoff.

================================================================================
A. ROLE ARCHITECTURE
================================================================================

Six roles. ONE shared AI engine. The role is read from the authenticated
server-side session (ctx.user.userRole). `ai.chat` has NO role field in its
input schema; zod strips unknown keys, so a client cannot assert a role it does
not hold. Both spoofing routes are tested: a role smuggled into the request
body, and a role asserted inside the conversation.

  ROLE              WORKFLOW                                        TOOLS  ACTIONS
  ----------------  ----------------------------------------------  -----  -------
  Customer          plan > estimate > find > compare > decide          8       5
  Contractor        findOpportunity > analyze > estimate > quote >     8       5
                    execute
  Engineer          analyze > verify > qaqc > manageRisk               8       4
  Architect         design > specify > coordinate > source             8       4
  Supplier          findDemand > matchProducts > quote > sell          8       5
  Project Manager   plan > track > control > report                    8       4

Eight tools per role, as the brief specifies. Every action href is asserted
against App.tsx, so a route typo is a compile-or-test failure, not a dead link.

DELIBERATELY ABSENT: a "Compare Providers" action. BuildHub has no comparison
surface - no route, no component, no endpoint. Shipping the button would be the
decorative control the brief forbids. Recorded as owner decision D4.

PROJECT CONTEXT is built on three refusals:
  - Authorization before retrieval. projectId is a SELECTOR among rows the
    caller may already see. Naming someone else's project yields "not among the
    ones you may see", and the block says outright: do NOT substitute another.
  - A provider never receives budget or spend. projects.directory withholds
    them; the AI path does not become the way around that.
  - Ask, don't guess. Multiple projects plus a vague question returns a question
    with the titles offered. A question with no project bearing costs NO QUERY.

THE COVERAGE NUMBER IS SMALL AND THAT IS A DELIBERATE POSITION, NOT AN OMISSION.
Part 14 lists roughly seventy domains; Part 14 also says "do NOT create shallow
filler documents merely to inflate coverage numbers." Those two instructions
pull against each other and the code resolves them explicitly: a paragraph per
domain would be material the model already knows, dressed as authoritative
BuildHub knowledge - which is worse than nothing, because it launders general
knowledge into apparent authority. What earns a document is knowledge specific
enough that a general answer would be materially worse. The assistant is told
which domains have documents, so it does not imply BuildHub has a position
where it has none.

  THE CORPUS IS NOT COMPLETE AND IS NOT DESCRIBED AS COMPLETE.
  Every document is tier 6. There is no tier 1-5 primary-source content.
  Expanding it with genuine higher-authority material is real remaining work.

PRECEDENCE CHAIN (five levels): attachment > BuildHub documented behaviour >
live authorized records > current/regulatory > general expertise. Stated as
"where two sources disagree, the EARLIER one wins - a precedence chain, not a
list of things you have available."

Four documented false premises are corrected from what the code does, including
the honest other half: an approved provider is NOT restricted to their declared
categories when QUOTING (though opening a qualified enquiry does require one).

F. MARKETPLACE
================================================================================

  Provider search:   BuildHub-listed providers first, always
  Ranking:           deterministic, server-side, over real fields only
  Match quality:     EXACT / PARTIAL / RELATED / NO-MATCH
  BuildHub priority: enforced - external only when nothing suitable exists or
                     the user explicitly asks
  External fallback: labelled "External recommendation - not currently listed

A partial match is never presented as exact. Terms BuildHub could not map are
carried forward and named, so a role-only match cannot be framed as exact - the
fix for a real staging answer where "swimming pool specialist in Aswan" matched
a generic contractor and reported EXACT.

No fabricated attributes: no invented rating, availability, specialization or
experience. The block names what BuildHub does NOT know and forbids estimating
it.

G. TRANSACTION ENGINE                                              (NEW)
================================================================================

server/opportunity.ts

  Opportunity detection:  intent classifier (phrases + patterns) AND the
                          server-side role. A homeowner asking about "available
                          projects" gets nothing.
  Role-specific:          contractors and suppliers get ranked open RFQs
  Scoring:                deterministic, server-side, named weights
  Confirmation:           carried explicitly per action
  Execution:              NONE - see below
  Audit:                  not implemented (PART P)

THE ARCHITECTURAL DECISION, which is the whole safety argument:

  THE AI HAS NO EXECUTE PATH.

Nothing in the module opens an enquiry, spends a credit, or submits a
quotation. It emits a PREPARED action - a real route and an id - and the person
confirms by going there, where the existing procedure runs with its existing
approval check, declared-category eligibility, monthly allowance and credit
accounting. "The AI must never become a transaction authorization bypass" is
not enforced by a check that could be forgotten; there is nothing to bypass
with. The PreparedAction type has no 'completed' variant, and a test asserts no
literal sneaks past it via a cast.

The five states the brief distinguishes are split across that boundary:

  RECOMMENDATION -> MATCH -> PREPARED ACTION | CONFIRMED ACTION -> COMPLETED
                                             ^
                                    the engine stops here

Authorization precedes retrieval: a role with no RFQ opportunities causes NO
QUERY, not a filtered one. The projection deliberately omits description,
budget, productReference and requesterId - the columns openQualifiedEnquiry
charges a credit to reveal. Ranking a feed must not become a way to read the
part of it that is paid for.

Match quality reflects a CONSEQUENCE: opening a qualified enquiry requires a
declared category, so anything outside them is RELATED and the block states
plainly that the provider cannot open it as things stand.

L. SECURITY
================================================================================

UNTRUSTED CONTENT (new this phase). The assembled prompt is one string, and it
mixed text BuildHub wrote with text other people wrote, unmarked. v.name,
v.location and v.categories were raw interpolations. A supplier could name
themselves across several lines and close the candidate block early. Two
defences:

  MECHANICAL - neutralizeUntrusted removes what text needs in order to POSE as
  structure: its own line breaks (including U+2028/U+2029, which are real line
  terminators), the === delimiter even inline, and the invisible characters
  that hide a payload from whoever reviews the listing. It does NOT guess at
  malicious phrasing - a blocklist of "ignore previous instructions" is a guess
  about wording, and wording is the cheapest thing to vary. The words survive;
  the structure does not, so the model can still REPORT a hostile listing.

  DECLARED - UNTRUSTED_CONTENT_RULE enters the system prompt after the
  precedence chain, phrased as a property of the channel, and closes the
  loophole that matters: not "ignore previous instructions" but a payload
  dressed as a system message or a note from the developers.

Channels covered: vendor name, vendor location, vendor categories, RFQ title,
attachment filename. Arabic payloads behave identically.

PRIVACY. server/aiPromptPrivacy.test.ts builds the WHOLE assembled prompt from
a poisoned viewer and asserts none of these survives, in both languages:
bcrypt hash, OpenAI key, JWT secret, database URL, SMTP password, S3 secret,
invitation token, session cookie, email, phone, username, openId.

A MUTATION SURVIVED AND CHANGED THE RULE: interpolating viewer.email passed,
because the forbidden list held only secrets. PII to a third-party provider is
a cost with no benefit; contact details are now forbidden too, and the POSITIVE
half is pinned so a privacy rule that also stripped the useful context fails.

Authorization precedes retrieval everywhere. No admin-internal information
reaches the model.

MUTATION TESTING CHANGED THE CODE, NOT JUST THE TESTS. Recorded because the
record is the point:

  - A recency weight worth 5 points could be set to 0 OR to 500 without
    changing a single outcome: the match-quality bucket or the tiebreak had
    already decided every case. A weight that cannot change a result makes the
    scoring look more considered than it is. Removed from the score, kept as a
    REASON, and the tiebreak now orders on createdAt instead of letting id
    stand in for "newest".
  - Two ranking tests were then unfalsifiable for the same reason - the exact
    and partial buckets are homogeneous by construction, so the weights never
    decide there. They decide in the RELATED bucket, which is where the tests
    now exercise them, with ids chosen so the tiebreak cannot reproduce the
    right order by coincidence (which is how the first rewrite passed).
  - Deleting the newline pass from neutralizeUntrusted left all 26 injection
    assertions green: every newline in the payload sat beside other whitespace,
    so the whitespace-collapse absorbed it. A lone \n between two word
    characters survives that collapse and is now its own test.
  - Two real source gaps surfaced from test failures rather than review: the
    RFQ title was neutralised at only one of two entry points, and the intent
    cues missed "find me finishing projects" because an adjective sits between
    the verb and the noun.

      calls". Named here rather than implied to be covered.

  L3  LIVE PROMPT-INJECTION BEHAVIOUR IS UNPROVEN. The MECHANICAL half is
      proven - untrusted text cannot forge prompt structure, and that half
      holds without the model cooperating. Whether a live model REFUSES a
      payload that survives neutralisation needs a real provider call per
      payload and has not been observed.

  L4  KNOWLEDGE CORPUS IS TIER 6 ONLY. Ten documents, all synthesised. No
      primary-source tier 1-5 content. Genuine remaining work.

  L5  MULTI-FILE ATTACHMENT ANALYSIS is not implemented. The transport
